# A matching and a weighted independence bound for Havel–Hakimi residue

Lean verifies Written on the Wall II, conjecture 61 for every finite nontrivial connected simple graph. The proof is in `Wowii61/Main.lean`; the statement in `Challenge.lean` is the formal-conjectures statement verbatim.

**Lemma.** Let U induce a perfect matching in a finite graph G. There is an independent set S outside U such that
R(G) ≤ |S| + ceil(|U∖N(S)|/2).

Delete the matching edges to form the auxiliary graph F, so U is stable. While F has edges, assign every remaining vertex its current degree in F, plus one if it is in U. Choose a vertex with largest score, preferring vertices outside U on ties. If an outside vertex is chosen, delete it. If a marked vertex is chosen, delete as a batch *all* marked vertices of this largest score. Such a batch has score at least two: every remaining edge touches an outside vertex, whose score is at least one, and ties favor outside vertices. Deleting a marked vertex leaves the degrees of the other marked vertices unchanged. Record the ordered batches and the outside deletions. At termination the remaining outside set S is independent and has no neighbor among the surviving marked vertices T.

Order U by its batch of deletion, put T last, and select any order within each group. Change the original matching on U to pair consecutive elements of this list. This preserves every degree. Replay the recorded deletions in this new realization, always deleting a vertex of actual maximum degree. An outside step is legal because its degree is exactly its auxiliary score and every marked degree is at most its score. At the start of a marked batch of score d, all outsiders and all markers outside the batch have actual degree at most d−1, and this cannot increase during the batch. A marker in the batch has actual degree d as long as its partner is present, and d−1 otherwise, since its auxiliary base degree does not change during this batch. Delete vertices in the batch in order of decreasing *current* degree, until the batch is empty. This is legal even if the choices differ from the arbitrary order used to pair the markers.

The remaining graph consists of S isolated vertices, and the matching on T truncated at the boundary of a suffix of a list paired consecutively. Among the vertices of T at most one has lost its partner. Its independence number is therefore |S|+ceil(|T|/2). For any maximum-degree deletion, residue cannot decrease: the ordinary deletion majorizes the HH derivative and residue is increasing under majorization of graphic lists of equal sum (Favaron–Mahéo–Saclé, or Griggs–Kleitman). Equivalently one may apply the classical Maxine algorithm bound directly, continuing on the residual matching. Hence R(G)≤|S|+ceil(|T|/2)≤|S|+ceil(|U∖N(S)|/2). This proves the lemma.

**Conjecture 61.** Let D be the diameter and m=ceil(D/3). On a diametral geodesic mark adjacent pairs at positions (0,1),(3,4),…,(3m−3,3m−2). Its length is at least 3m−2, and it is isometric on all subintervals. The set U of 2m markers induces just the matching of these pairs. In the marker order, every outside vertex has at most two marker neighbors, and any two are consecutive: nonconsecutive markers have geodesic distance at least three.

Apply the lemma, and let B=U∩N(S). Split B according to odd and even positions in marker order. Delete the smaller part from U, and take the resulting markers together with S in the *original* graph. Each vertex of S has at most one retained neighbor: every marker neighbor lies in B, and any two have opposite parities. The retained markers induce a subgraph of a matching. Thus the selected graph is a forest, of order at least
|S|+2m−floor(|B|/2) = m+|S|+ceil((2m−|B|)/2) ≥ m+R(G).
This is the exact mathematical inequality including the ceiling.

The Lean proof establishes the needed numerical deletion theorem from the repository’s Havel–Hakimi recursion. It uses degree lists to implement the batch replay and constructs the two candidate forests in the original graph. Run `lake build` in this repository; the final theorem depends only on `propext`, `Classical.choice` and `Quot.sound`.

The matching lemma was motivated by 371,754,972 relevant marked matchings in all 12,293,433 unlabeled graphs of orders 2–10, and 9,600,000 larger planted-matching proposals. These checks are independent of the batch replay proof. The original partial reductions at diameter four and on triangle-free graphs are no longer needed for this proof.


**Literature and status.** The classical maximum-degree deletion result used above is the Maxine bound, proved in [Favaron, Mahéo and Saclé (1991), Theorem 3.3 and its referee remark](https://doi.org/10.1002/jgt.3190150107) and [Griggs and Kleitman (1994)](https://doi.org/10.1016/0012-365X(92)00479-B). The exact statement is from the [formal-conjectures library](https://google-deepmind.github.io/formal-conjectures/src/FormalConjectures/WrittenOnTheWallII/GraphConjecture61/). Searches of its issues by conjecture number and exact filename, the WOWII catalog, the literature on residue and induced forests, and [the public WOWII proof campaign](https://github.com/Kuberwastaken/c5-k4) located partial results but no complete proof of 61. A DataCite title search also found Haoyu Chen’s August 2026 preprint. It proves the bound for six specified diameters and gives further partial results at diameter four. Its abstract explicitly leaves the general conjecture open. This search found no earlier full proof. [Chen, Partial Results on the WOWII-61 Graffiti Conjecture](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.22069069)
